# Hero B/C Polish — Master Prompt

## Mission
Make Hero B (terminal-cute) and Hero C (shoujo manga) genuinely polished alternatives to Hero A in the existing `lyra-on.top` repository. **Hero A is the quality benchmark and must remain unchanged.** Preserve B's terminal concept and C's manga-panel concept; do not turn either into a copy of A.

## Read first
Check `git status` and follow `AGENTS.md`, `docs/dev/ai/master-context.md`, `docs/dev/ai/orchestrator-prompt.md`, `docs/CONTRACT.md`, `docs/RECON.md`, and `STATE.md` (plus applicable task/rule files). Use only the project's public-safe context; never read private/global context or secrets. Follow the repository's documented Orca/worktree/task workflow and file-ownership contract.

## Execute now
Immediately dispatch independent, non-overlapping implementation tasks for B and C, plus a visual QA/review task. Give each task explicit file ownership, acceptance criteria, and verification steps; verify that workers actually start. Continue implementing, reviewing diffs, integrating, and iterating—**not just planning or reporting progress**.

- **Hero B:** Fix the actual layout/cascade defects. The terminal must feel like a deliberately composed hero, not a full-width empty box; balance readable terminal copy, name/tagline, WhiskerFrame mascot, and prominent CTAs. Own the required grid, spacing, sizing, and responsive behavior in B's implementation.
- **Hero C:** Make the manga concept visibly intentional: a coherent, tightly composed panel grid, clear visual hierarchy, legible speech bubbles, visible panel treatments, mascot, and useful CTAs. Investigate why its intended stylesheet/panel styling is absent or ineffective before layering on more decoration.
- **Both:** Stay dense, bright, sparkly, and playful—cute but not childish; never corporate, generic, or accidentally sparse. Follow the palette, type, motifs, motion, contrast, accessibility, and honest-copy rules in `docs/dev/ai/master-context.md`. Reuse approved content and routes; do not invent personal facts. Keep both concepts distinct.

## Visual acceptance gate
Inspect the current templates and CSS, then capture **fresh browser screenshots** for A, B, and C at **360px and 1280px**. Compare B/C against A at identical sizes and iterate until composition, spacing, hierarchy, and polish are intentional. Confirm no giant dead zones, clipped/overlapping content, horizontal overflow, missing assets/styles, or browser-console errors; confirm keyboard/focus and reduced-motion behavior remain sound. Inspect rendered output rather than trusting source code or old screenshots alone.

## Verification and safety
Run `just screenshots` and `just check`, plus any focused browser checks needed; verify the actual frozen preview as well as template screenshots. Fix relevant failures and rerun checks. Truthfully distinguish pre-existing failures and checks not run. Preserve user changes; never reset/stash/delete work to get a clean tree. Stage specific files only, respect shared-file ownership, and do not push, deploy, or publish. Record and restore the original `content/site.yaml` `hero_variant` after comparisons unless the operator explicitly requests a new default.

## Done means
B and C both look deliberate and responsive at mobile and desktop; A remains untouched; `just check` and visual/browser checks have evidence-backed results; and `STATE.md`/the project's required handoff records the changes, screenshot paths, exact commands/results, remaining defects, and blockers. Keep working until this gate is met or a concrete blocker is documented.
